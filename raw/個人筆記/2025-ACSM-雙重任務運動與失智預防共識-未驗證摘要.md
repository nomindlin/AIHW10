# International Consensus on Dual-Task Exercise and Brain Health for Dementia Prevention

## 1. Core Consensus Overview
This consensus statement defines clinical protocols for **Simultaneous Cognitive-Motor Training (Dual-Task Exercise)** to prevent Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) and dementia. It demonstrates that combining physical exercise with concurrent cognitive challenges produces superior improvements in executive function, processing speed, and episodic memory compared to physical exercise or cognitive training performed separately.

## 2. Key Physiological Mechanisms
* **Guided Plasticity Facilitation Hypothesis:** Physical exercise acts as a physiological "primer" by elevating circulating **Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF)**, Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF), and IGF-1, stimulating neurogenesis and synaptogenesis in the **dentate gyrus of the hippocampus**. Simultaneous cognitive engagement then "guides" the functional integration and survival of these newly formed neural circuits.
* **Prefrontal Cortex (PFC) & Executive Network Recruitment:** Dual-tasking forces simultaneous activation and structural connectivity between the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), supplementary motor area, and basal ganglia, improving neural efficiency under divided-attention demands.
* **Cerebrovascular Reactivity & Glymphatic Clearance:** Aerobic exertion enhances cerebral blood flow (CBF), nitric oxide-mediated endothelial dilation, and beta-amyloid clearance.

## 3. FITT-VP Exercise Prescription
* **Frequency:** 3 days/week of structured dual-task sessions.
* **Intensity:**
  * *Physical Component:* Moderate aerobic intensity (60–75% HRmax; RPE 12–14) or moderate resistance/stepping loads.
  * *Cognitive Component:* Moderate-to-high cognitive load ("sweet spot" where dual-task cost slows gait speed by ~10–20% without causing motor breakdown or loss of balance).
* **Time:** 40–60 minutes per session (minimum 20–30 minutes of continuous or interval dual-task engagement) for at least 12–24 weeks.
* **Type:** Simultaneous motor + cognitive tasks (avoid sequential/separate blocks):
  * *Motor:* Marching in place, multi-directional stepping (square-stepping), brisk walking, or cycling.
  * *Cognitive:* Verbal fluency (naming categories while stepping), serial subtraction (counting backward by 3s or 7s), Stroop color-word inhibition drills, or spatial memory recall.
* **Volume:** 3–4 dual-task blocks of 5–8 minutes each per session, separated by 2-minute active recovery intervals.
* **Progression:** Increase cognitive complexity (switching from simple recall to executive inhibition/task-switching) and motor unpredictability (directional cues) systematically every 2–3 weeks.

## 4. Clinical Takeaways
* **Simultaneous > Sequential:** Performing cognitive tasks *during* physical movement yields significantly greater prefrontal cortex activation and transfer to real-world fall prevention than doing a workout followed by brain games.
* **Dual-Task Cost as a Biomarker:** Measuring "Dual-Task Cost" (percentage drop in walking speed when performing a cognitive task) serves as both a sensitive early screening tool for MCI/fall risk and an objective metric to track training progression.
* **Social & Enjoyment Adherence:** Group-based dual-task formats (e.g., rhythm stepping, interactive cue games) amplify psychosocial engagement and long-term adherence in older adults with subjective cognitive decline.
