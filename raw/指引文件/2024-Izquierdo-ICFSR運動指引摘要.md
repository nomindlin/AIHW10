# ICFSR International Clinical Practice Guidelines on Physical Activity and Exercise for Older Adults

## 1. Core Consensus Overview
The International Conference on Frailty and Sarcopenia Research (ICFSR) Task Force establishes evidence-based clinical guidelines prioritizing **multicomponent exercise programs**—specifically integrating **muscle power training** (high-velocity concentric movements)—as the primary non-pharmacological intervention to prevent, delay, and reverse frailty and sarcopenia in older adults.

## 2. Key Physiological Mechanisms
* **Type II Muscle Fiber Recruitment & Rate of Force Development (RFD):** Aging disproportionately causes atrophy of fast-twitch (Type IIa/IIx) muscle fibers, leading to a steeper decline in muscle power (~3–4% per year after age 60) than maximal strength (~1–2% per year). High-velocity concentric contractions restore neuromuscular firing rates and motor unit recruitment.
* **Neuromuscular Junction (NMJ) Preservation:** Explosive intent during resistance movements preserves alpha-motor neuron innervation and reduces denervation-reinnervation remodeling deficits.
* **Systemic Inflammatory & Anabolic Signaling:** Downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-$\alpha$) while stimulating mTORC1-mediated muscle protein synthesis (MPS) and IGF-1 signaling.

## 3. FITT-VP Exercise Prescription
* **Frequency:** 2–3 non-consecutive days/week for resistance/power; daily integration for balance and mobility.
* **Intensity:**
  * *Strength/Hypertrophy:* 70–80% 1RM (RPE 15–17 on Borg 6–20 scale).
  * *Power Training:* 40–60% 1RM performed with **maximal intentional velocity** during the concentric phase and controlled (2–3 seconds) eccentric lowering.
* **Time:** 45–60 minutes per multicomponent session (including warm-up, power/resistance, balance, and aerobic components).
* **Type:** Multicomponent regimen combining lower-limb functional power exercises (e.g., rapid chair stands, leg press), progressive resistance training (PRT), dynamic balance, and gait training.
* **Volume:** 1–3 sets of 8–12 repetitions per major muscle group (6–8 exercises per session).
* **Progression:** Progress movement velocity first at light-to-moderate loads before increasing external resistance; advance to unstable surfaces or reduced base of support for balance.

## 4. Clinical Takeaways
* **Power > Strength Alone:** Muscle power is a stronger predictor of functional independence, stair-climbing speed, and fall recovery than maximal isometric strength.
* **Frail & Pre-Frail Stratification:** Even severely frail or institutionalized older adults respond safely to progressive resistance and power training when initiated at low loads (30–40% 1RM) with proper supervision.
* **Order of Execution:** Perform high-velocity power and balance drills early in the session while the central nervous system is unfatigued.
